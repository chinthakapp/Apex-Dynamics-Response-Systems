# Method 01 Stakeholder Map

## Primary stakeholders

- Chief negotiator
  - Role: leads the negotiation strategy and decides whether to pursue diplomacy, diversion, or deterrence
  - Influence: high
  - Interest: very high
- Kaiju translator
  - Role: interprets Kaiju signals, communication patterns, and intent
  - Influence: high
  - Interest: very high
- City leadership / emergency command
  - Role: decides public safety, evacuation, and city priorities
  - Influence: high
  - Interest: high
- Kaiju leadership or representative group
  - Role: potential counterpart for negotiation or motive discovery
  - Influence: high
  - Interest: high

## Secondary stakeholders

- Civil defense / military / security teams
  - Role: manage response, containment, and threat deterrence
  - Influence: medium-high
  - Interest: high
- Population / city residents
  - Role: affected users and witnesses of attacks
  - Influence: medium
  - Interest: high
- Infrastructure operators
  - Role: maintain power, transit, and utilities during attacks
  - Influence: medium
  - Interest: high
- Public communications / morale teams
  - Role: shape message, reduce panic, and maintain trust
  - Influence: medium
  - Interest: high

## Hidden or adjacent stakeholders

- Ecology / research experts
  - Role: may explain Kaiju behavior, territory patterns, and attraction signals
  - Influence: medium
  - Interest: medium
- Legal / treaty / political negotiators
  - Role: may define acceptable negotiation terms and boundaries
  - Influence: medium
  - Interest: medium
- Urban planners / land-use teams
  - Role: could help redirect Kaiju attention or reduce vulnerable zones
  - Influence: medium
  - Interest: medium

## Initial stakeholder insight

The project is not only about stopping attacks; it is also about understanding the root cause of Kaiju interest and creating a path that redirects attention toward a safer or more productive outcome.
