# Method 01 Scope Boundaries

## Original request

"Im want to negociate with Kaiju to find out why they are attacking the city and diver that to something productive, i still do not know yet. i want to avioid the attack and find a bait to divert their interest and stop happening this again ever"

## In scope

- Understanding why Kaiju are attacking the city
- Identifying what the Kaiju are responding to or seeking
- Finding a way to redirect their interest away from the city
- Creating a negotiation or diversion strategy that reduces repeat attacks
- Defining constraints on safety, public trust, and operational feasibility

## Out of scope for now

- Final combat strategy or full military response planning
- Long-term city rebuilding or permanent relocation planning
- Specific technical implementation of a system without validating the underlying problem first
- A single “magic” bait without understanding Kaiju motives

## Known facts

- Kaiju are attacking the city.
- The team wants to negotiate or understand the cause of the attacks.
- The team wants to avoid future attacks by redirecting interest.
- The user has a broad objective, but not yet a clear root cause or target intervention.

## Assumptions to test

- Kaiju are reacting to something in or around the city.
- The attacks are not random and may be tied to resource, territory, or emotional triggers.
- A diversion strategy may work better than a purely defensive one.
- A productive outcome for both sides may exist if the actual motive is discovered.

## Open questions

- What is the Kaiju objective: territory, resource, warning, migration, or threat response?
- What patterns already exist before an attack?
- Which city elements are attracting Kaiju attention?
- Who can confirm intent, and how do we gather that evidence?
- What would an acceptable “productive” alternative look like for both sides?

## Summary

This project is currently a fluid problem. The user has a broad goal of reducing attacks through understanding and redirection, but the root cause and desired diversion mechanism are still not yet defined. The next step is to narrow the problem into a specific cause pattern and identify which stakeholders and signals matter most.
